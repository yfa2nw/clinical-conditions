## Request 1: Overhaul of USMLE quizzes

It has been decided that the USMLE quizzes need to be deprecated, replaced by new quizzes that are similar in structure, tone, and style compared to the ones in the neighboring "review" folder (which we will call them "sample quizzes", there should be 360 questions in there).

The replaced quizzes will have 2 main functions:

1. Serve as an anki-like (short, concise, large-in-volume, increasing familiarity) review for the content for each page, spanning from pathophysiology to management options.

Some notes regarding the new replacement quizzes:

- As mentioned, they will be modeled after the sample quizzes in the neighboring "review" file.
- Replacement is not necessary if the old questions fit the style of the new ones
- If the sample quizzes contain relevant questions for a specific page, you can borrow those questions from the sample quizzes directly to the corresponding page with a few tweaks in the explanations section
- The quizzes are not meant to be tricky, so there shouldn't be traps (e.g., intentionally vague wording, wordplay, unreasonably long options with one minor mistake); however, distractors/red herrings can be used
- Memory questions are designed to be short, concise, and almost relied on reflexes - the "what" and "which" questions (e.g., What type of tumor does Barrett esophagus increase the risk of? Which of the following is the most common cause of infectious esophagitis? Which of the following is not an extrahepatic manifestation of chronic HBV or HCV infection? Which of the following histological features would make you point towards a Crohn disease diagnosis rather than an ulcerative colitis one? What type of cell is responsible for producing myelin in spinal cord axons?) (this is not an exhaustive list - it's to provide an idea of what it can be like)
- Reasoning questions are designed to rely more on reasoning - the "what is the mechanism behind it", "what do to next", "why", and "how" questions (e.g., A patient presents with a 2-day history of bloody, mucoid, and small-volume diarrhea - what is the physiological mechanism behind their presentation? Why does the typical presentation of appendicitis involve migrating pain from periumbilical to RLQ? A patient on H. pylori triple therapy returns to the clinic reporting severe allergic reactions to amoxicillin - what is the next best step? Given [a slew of presentations], which of the following presentations or lab findings would most likely confirm your differential?) (this is not an exhaustive list - it's to provide an idea of what it can be like)
- Analysis questions are passage based, or require data interpretation; they rely on inference, connecting the dots, and assumes familiarity of the page content (e.g., a patient presents to the ED with a 7-day history of dysentery and vomiting caused by consumption of undercooked beef mince. They recently observed frothy urine and have decreased urine output. [provide blood test results that suggest thrombocytopenia and hemolytic anemia]. What would the pathogen most likely be? / What would the most appropriate management strategy be for this patient? a patient with chronic alcohol addiction presents with mild epigastric pain with [provide LFT results that suggest chronic pancreatitis]. Which test should you most likely do next?) (this is not an exhaustive list - it's to provide an idea of what it can be like)
- I would like each non-special topic clinical page to have 25 questions - 10 memory ones, 10 reasoning ones, and 5 analysis ones, covering hopefully most information in each page
- For each special topic clinical page, there will be 40 questions - 15 memory, 15 reasoning, and 10 analysis ones
- For each non-pharmacology physiology page, there will be 25 questions - 10 memory, 10 reasoning, and 5 analysis ones
- For each pharmacology page, there will be 15 questions - 6 memory, 6 reasoning, 3 analysis ones

Some notes regarding the explanations for each question:

- For each incorrect option, explain why they are incorrect or, alternatively, explain in which scenario it would be considered correct

For the metabolic acidosis and alkalosis page:

- discuss the different types of metabolic acidosis (NAGMA and AGMA) and how they are differentiated (anion gap calculation)
- discuss the pathophysiology of NAGMA and AGMA - NAGMA roughly being renal/GI loss of bicarbonate, while AGMA roughly being an increase in organic acids
- discuss the different types of NAGMA renal causes, which can be divided into the ones that lower GFR (CKD), and ones that feature normal GFR (RTA I, II, and IV); detailed explanation on what each cause is is not necessary, but do mention their typical blood/urinalysis findings so we can differentiate them, especially for the different types of renal tubular acidosis (e.g., potassium levels and urine pH); also briefly discuss the different types of NAGMA GI causes, which includes diarrhea and pancreatic fistulas - an in-depth discussion is not warranted because they are explained in detail in the GI system
- discuss the different types of AGMA causes, which includes diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA), uremic acidosis, lactic acidosis, and toxic alcohol ingestion; for each AGMA type, mention their typical blood/urinalysis findings so we can differentiate them (for toxic alcohol ingestion, do mention how to calculate the osmotic gap) as well as their respective pathophysiologies

- briefly discuss the relation between proton and potassium exchange in the bloodstream and exceptions to this relation
- discuss the pathophysiology of metabolic alkalosis (e.g., renal and GI loss of protons lead to increased bicarbonate levels)

- discuss the symptoms and complications of metabolic alkalosis (not limited to increased free albumin-induced tetany, hypokalemia-induced arrhythmias, and on rarer occassions, compensatory hypoventilation, etc.)
- discuss the flowchart for diagnosis (and finding out the root cause) of metabolic acidosis/alkalosis; if delta-delta ratio is part of the diagnostic flowchart, include a brief explanation of what it is
- discuss the treatment strategies for different causes of metabolic acidosis (treat the root cause) and alkalosis
