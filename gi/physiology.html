Gastric Secretion

Overview

Food triggers gastric secretion in three overlapping phases, which start it, sustain it, and then slow it down.

  • Cephalic phaseAbout 30% of HCl; starts secretion before food arrives
  • Gastric phaseAbout 60% of HCl; food in the stomach sustains secretion
  • Intestinal phaseAbout 10% of HCl; chyme in the duodenum mostly slows secretion

Cells and signals

  • 1Mucous neck cellsMucus and HCO3−, which protect the mucosa
  • 2Parietal cellsHCl and IF
  • 3Chief cellsPepsinogen and gastric lipase
  • 4ECL cellsHistamine
  • 5G cellsGastrin
  • 6D cellsSomatostatin

How a parietal cell makes HCl

Cephalic phase

  1. Sight, smell, taste, and chewingOr the thought of food
  2. CN XVagal output to the stomach
  3. Secretion startsBefore food arrives

Cells activated

  • 2Parietal cellsACh → increased HCl
  • 3Chief cellsACh → increased pepsinogen
  • 4ECL cellsACh → increased histamine
  • 5G cellsGRP → increased gastrin
  • 6D cellsACh → decreased somatostatin

Note: This phase depends on CN X, so vagotomy abolishes it.

Gastric phase

Triggers

  • DistensionCN X and enteric reflexes release ACh
  • Peptides and amino acidsG cells release gastrin
  • Food buffering HClHigher pH decreases somatostatin

Main route to HCl

  1. GastrinFrom G cells
  2. HistamineFrom ECL cells
  3. HClFrom parietal cells, through H2 receptors

Other cells

  • 3Chief cellsIncreased pepsinogen, which HCl converts to pepsin
  • 6D cellsOnce pH falls below 3, increased somatostatin inhibits G, ECL, and parietal cells

Low pH is the brake on gastrin, so losing HCl raises gastrin.

Intestinal phase

Stimulation

  • Peptides in the duodenumDuodenal G cells release gastrin, causing a brief rise

Slowing secretion

  • AcidS cells release secretin → decreased gastrin and HCl; increased pancreatic HCO3−
  • FatI cells release CCK and K cells release GIP → decreased HCl and slower gastric emptying
  • Distension or hyperosmolar chymeEnterogastric reflex → decreased HCl and slower gastric emptying

Note: Slowing the stomach gives the duodenum time to neutralize and digest chyme.

When it fails

Too much HCl

  • Zollinger-Ellison syndromeGastrin from a tumor ignores feedback → increased HCl
  • H. pyloriIncreased gastrin → increased HCl
  • NSAIDs and alcoholCOX inhibition → decreased PG → increased HCl and decreased mucus
  • Cushing ulcersIncreased ICP → increased CN X activity → increased HCl
  • GERDHCl enters the esophagus

Too little HCl

  • Autoimmune gastritisParietal cell loss → decreased HCl and IF; no feedback → increased gastrin

Where drugs act

  • PPIsIrreversibly block the H+/K+ ATPase
  • H2R antagonistsBlock H2 receptors on parietal cells
  • MisoprostolProtects the mucosa in place of lost PG

Digestion and Absorption

Overview

Enzymes break food down in the lumen and at the brush border, and enterocytes absorb the products, mainly in the duodenum and jejunum.

  • 1MouthSalivary amylase and lingual lipase
  • 2StomachHCl, pepsin, and gastric lipase
  • 3PancreasAmylase, proteases, lipase, and HCO3−
  • 4BileBile salts, which emulsify fat
  • 5Brush borderFinal enzymes and transporters on enterocytes

Carbohydrates

Digestion

  • 1MouthSalivary amylase starts on starch; stomach acid stops it
  • 3PancreasPancreatic amylase → maltose, maltotriose, and α-limit dextrins
  • 5Brush borderDisaccharidases → monosaccharides

Brush border enzymes

  • LactaseLactose → glucose and galactose
  • SucraseSucrose → glucose and fructose
  • MaltaseMaltose → glucose

Absorption

Note: Fiber is not digested, so colonic bacteria ferment it.

Proteins

Digestion

  • 2StomachHCl converts pepsinogen to pepsin → peptides
  • 3PancreasTrypsin, chymotrypsin, elastase, and carboxypeptidases → oligopeptides and amino acids
  • 5Brush borderPeptidases → amino acids, dipeptides, and tripeptides

Activating pancreatic proteases

  1. EnterokinaseOn the brush border
  2. TrypsinFrom trypsinogen
  3. Other proteasesTrypsin activates them, and more trypsinogen

Absorption

Enterokinase sits in the duodenum, so trypsin normally stays inactive until it leaves the pancreas.

Fats

Digestion

  • 1MouthLingual lipase, a minor role in adults
  • 2StomachGastric lipase, a minor role in adults
  • 4BileBile salts emulsify fat into small droplets
  • 3PancreasLipase and colipase → monoglycerides and fatty acids; also cholesterol esterase and phospholipase A2
  • 5Brush borderMicelles of bile salts deliver the products to enterocytes

Absorption

Bile salts recycle

  1. MicellesIn the jejunum
  2. Terminal ileumBile salts are reabsorbed
  3. Portal veinThey return to the liver

Note: Short- and medium-chain fatty acids skip chylomicrons and enter the portal vein directly.

Micronutrients

Iron

  1. ReductionFe3+ → Fe2+, helped by HCl and vitamin C
  2. Into the enterocyteThrough DMT1
  3. Into the bloodThrough ferroportin, then carried by transferrin

B12

  1. StomachBinds haptocorrin from saliva
  2. DuodenumProteases free it, and it binds IF from parietal cells
  3. Terminal ileumThe B12 and IF complex is absorbed
  4. BloodCarried by transcobalamin

Others

  • Fat-soluble vitaminsA, D, E, and K travel in micelles and leave in chylomicrons
  • Water-soluble vitaminsAbsorbed by transporters, mostly in the jejunum
  • FolateAbsorbed in the jejunum
  • CalciumAbsorbed in the duodenum; vitamin D increases absorption

Hepcidin is the brake on iron: it binds ferroportin and causes it to be degraded.

When it fails

Maldigestion

  • Lactose intoleranceUndigested lactose → osmotic diarrhea and gas from bacterial digestion
  • Pancreatic insufficiencyDecreased pancreatic enzymes → undigested fat, protein, and carbohydrate
  • Bile salt deficiencyDecreased micelles → steatorrhea and fat-soluble vitamin deficiencies
  • Zollinger-Ellison syndromeExcess acid inactivates pancreatic enzymes → diarrhea

Malabsorption

  • Celiac diseaseBlunted villi → decreased absorption, with iron and folate deficiency
  • Ileal Crohn diseaseDecreased ileal absorption → B12 deficiency and bile salt loss
  • Autoimmune gastritisDecreased IF → B12 deficiency

Other

  • HemochromatosisDecreased hepcidin → increased iron absorption
  • Acute pancreatitisTrypsin activated inside the pancreas → autodigestion
