# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## What this is

Clinical Conditions: a static study reference for medical conditions, organized by organ system, with a quiz on every page that reviews it from recall to reasoning, explaining every option. Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no dependencies, framework or build step.

## Commands

There is nothing to install or lint. The site itself has no build step; only its PDFs (review articles, practice exams, worksheets, and quiz copies) are generated. Open `index.html` in a browser. Everything must keep working from `file://`, so do not load content with `fetch` or ES modules.

- After changing anything, run `node tools/check-site.mjs` from the project root. It takes about 20 seconds, opening pages in up to 8 tabs at once (`TABS=<n>` changes that), and exits with a failure if a question bank or the markup is invalid, if correct options are too often the longest, or if any page has a console error, overflows sideways at desktop or phone width, or has a review popup that does not open and close. Add `--site <folder,folder>` to open only those systems in the browser (every file is still checked); after touching `shared/`, run it in full. Add `--shots <folder>` to save a full-page screenshot of every page, which is much slower, or add `--only <id,id>` as well to capture just those articles. It does not check content, so compare a new review article with its page by hand.
- Rebuild a review article PDF only when the site owner asks for it, even after changing a review article or diagram; say in the summary that the PDF is now out of date instead. Run from the project root: `node tools/build-review-pdf.mjs gi` or `node tools/build-review-pdf.mjs neuro`. It writes `gi/gi-review-articles.pdf` or `neuro/neuro-review-articles.pdf`, which the landing page links to, using headless Edge. The cover shows "Last updated" with the build date, and a References section at the end gathers each page's `section.references`, grouped by review; it is left out when no page has references. It also takes each page's diagrams, which the popups do not show, using the `aria-label` as the legend and the list beside the diagram as the key to its numbers; small card icons (`.lesion-map`) are left out. The pharmacology pages form their own "Pharmacology" group after the special topics, though they sit on the physiology page. Each diagram goes before the review section that matches the page section it sits in: Etiology before "Etiology and mechanisms", Treatment before "Management", and a physiology section before the review heading of the same name or the one sharing its key words. On a review organized by cause, it goes before the first heading after the previous standard section. A diagram in the first section, an Overview, or one with no match goes after Key points. `GI_FIGURE_SECTIONS` overrides this for the GI physiology pages, listing each diagram's section in page order, so update it if one of those pages gains or loses a diagram. `FIG_DEBUG=1` prints each placement that was not an exact match.
- Practice exams: `node tools/build-exam-pdf.mjs gi` or `node tools/build-exam-pdf.mjs neuro` writes `gi/gi-practice-exam.pdf` or `neuro/neuro-practice-exam.pdf` from `tools/exam-<system>-content.mjs`, written separately from the question banks, so changing a bank does not require rebuilding them. The landing page links to them from each system's shelf; they replaced the older practice tests, which were removed. In every PDF builder, any element wider than the 178 mm text width makes Edge shrink the whole document, so keep tables and figures within it. Each exam is a 150-minute final in three timed parts:
  - **Part 1, Foundations (45 minutes):** 10 easy and 10 moderate single-best questions worth 1 point each, shuffled, on anatomy, physiology, histology, embryology, and pharmacology (neurology leaves out embryology). Then 10 hard questions worth 2 points each, at the end in the order written, in varied formats: data interpretation, "Select 2", "least likely", justify-your-choice, ordering, calculation, short answer, and linked sets that share a passage or build on the first question (`set: n` on the first, `shared: true` on the rest).
  - **Part 2, Clinical scenarios (60 minutes):** five patients told in passages that can be long, with a full history, examination, and investigations. Each scenario has 5–8 questions: at least 2 multiple choice and 2 short answers (15 words or fewer), and any other types. Scenarios 1 and 4 are moderate, 2 and 3 hard, and 5 easy; the second is always the hardest and longest. Every passage after the first starts a new page with a "do not turn back" cue and the scenario's opening repeated, because later passages can answer earlier questions. Scenario headings name no condition, because questions often ask for the diagnosis.
  - **Part 3, Reading the research (45 minutes):** an open-access (CC BY) paper read through PubMed Central, with quoted excerpts, abridged tables, supplementary information from StatPearls, and 5 questions: an easy introduction, a moderate question, then 3 hard ones that apply course content to what the data can and cannot show. Its sources go in `PART3.references`.
  - **Hard questions** never rest on sheer recall: they ask for inference, connecting ideas across topics, or applying new information given in a passage. Mark invented data as illustrative.
  - **Question types** in the content files: `mc` (the default when `options` is present, with `select: 2` or `justify: <words>` plus `justifyAnswer` and `justifyMarking`), `sa` (with `words`, or `lines` and `work: true` for a calculation), and `order` (`items` and the correct `order` of their indexes). Every multiple choice question has five options, shuffled when built, so explanations and `notes` never name a letter. Difficulty appears only in the answers, apart from a whole-exam difficulty bar in the instructions. The builder prints the difficulty mix and formats, flags a scenario outside the rules and any answer over its word limit, and reports how often the correct option is longest (keep it near 1 in 5) and capitalized terms with no definition; add those to the content file's `EXTRA_TERMS`.
- Exam forms (replacing the practice exams): `node tools/build-form-pdf.mjs <gi|neuro> <a|b>` writes `<system>/<system>-exam-form-a.pdf` or `-form-b.pdf` from `tools/exam-<system>-form-a.mjs` or `-form-b.mjs`, linked from each system's shelf on the landing page. The older practice exam PDFs and content files stay on disk, unlinked, to mine for questions. Both Form Bs are complete; neither Form A has questions yet. GI's Form B was rebuilt after its content reorganization: its content file exports `PARTS`, which the builder lays out as five parts (the old four-part exam is `exam-gi-form-b-old.mjs`, unlinked), and neurology's still has the four-part layout below until its content is restructured. The two forms draw on the same pages but are separate exams, with no difficulty bar in the instructions; the covers signal the level without naming it (Form A light, an outlined letter, two gauge bars; Form B dark and hatched, a filled coral letter, every bar). The abbreviations page lists every abbreviation on the system's pages until questions are written, then only those the questions use. Questions are not written yet.
  - **Form A:** 100 single-best multiple choice questions in 120 minutes, 1 point each, with no headings: questions 1–70 mix 40 memory and 30 reasoning questions in random order, and 71–100 are analysis (cases and data, linked sets sharing a passage). A few may be "Select 2".
  - **GI Form B (five parts):** 200 minutes, 160 points, about 90 questions, following the curriculum below: Part 1 Foundations (45 minutes, 8 problems of 5, tagged by discipline), Part 2 Reasoning (40 minutes, 6 stations of 5, from the special topics), Part 3 Clinical Scenarios (60 minutes, 3 patients of 15 over 3 stages, two ending in a 5-point synthesis), Part 4 Pharmacology (20 minutes, 15 multiple-choice questions of 1 point, 3 for each of 5 patients), Part 5 Research Analysis (35 minutes, one CC BY paper of 30 points, now C-GALL). Headings are only Problem, Station, Scenario, or Decision with a number. The instructions show a coverage matrix from each set's `groups` (the builder warns of a group no set covers), an equation sheet comes before the abbreviations (`EQUATIONS`), and there is no formats page: `FORMAT_HINTS` prints one line where a format first appears. Formats include Mislead me, Split the pair, Read the graph, and What changed?; the `fields` type gives labeled answer lines. The builder checks points by part, word limits, and whether a correct option is longest. The site owner confirmed the curriculum version and its answers (November 2026), with infliximab without a thiopurine as the single answer for perianal Crohn disease after lymphoma.
  - **Form B (four parts, neurology):** 180 minutes, 140 points, in four parts. Part 1, Mechanisms and data (listed 60 minutes, designed for about 52): 10 basic-science problems of 5 points, each tagged by discipline, with at least one each of anatomy, histology, physiology, pharmacology, embryology, and pathophysiology, using formats such as prediction grids, pathways, sketches, and calculations. Part 2, Unfolding cases (60 minutes): 3 patients of 15 points over 3–4 stages, with no turning back, built on the clinical conditions and special topics. Part 3, Reading the research (listed 45, designed for about 40): one CC BY paper, 30 points. Part 4, Synthesis (15 minutes): one extended answer of 15 points, marked by a rubric. Every piece of data in a passage should be needed by a question, and most questions should need more than one; new diseases or drugs may appear when the passage supplies the material. Short answers carry word limits (15 words for 1 point, 25 for 2), multiple choice is a follow-up rather than a main type, and all answers follow the last part. Answers are marked by reasoning step. A "Question formats" page after the instructions lists the formats by part (`FORMAT_GROUPS` in the builder); add a format there when a question needs a new one.
- Worksheets: `node tools/build-worksheet-pdf.mjs <gi|neuro|foundations|msk|resp|cardio|endocrine|renal|review> <page id>` writes `<system>/worksheets/<id>.pdf` from `tools/worksheets/<system>/<id>.mjs`. An article with `data-worksheet="worksheets/<id>.pdf"` gets a Worksheet link in its header, added by `app.js`. Rebuild a worksheet after changing its content file. Worksheets exist for every GI condition and special topic page, the renal pages Metabolic acidosis and Metabolic alkalosis, and the neurology pages Localizing a lesion, Ruling out mimics, Stroke symptoms and complications, Motor signs and syndromes, and Acute ischemic stroke. Intracerebral and subarachnoid hemorrhage share one, `intracerebral-subarachnoid-hemorrhage`, linked from both articles: a `covers` list of page titles adds a note at the start, and its Part 1 section names start with the page.
  - **Layout:** no cover; the header shows the page title and "Last updated". The look matches the practice exams but is kept light and open: questions are separated by white space rather than rules, two-column pages have no column rule, small headings have no trailing line, answer lines are faint and dotted, and tables use soft alternating shading instead of row lines. Keep it that way when adding formats.
  - **Flowchart (page 1):** a `flowchart` object maps the whole page on one page: a `top` box, a grid of `columns` (one per cause or type) down the `rows` (for a condition: Cause, Why it happens, EGD and biopsy or the main test, Treatment), a `span` band that applies to every column, and `chains` of consequences. About 15–20 key terms are `[[answer]]` blanks, and the completed flowchart opens the answers. Columns can be causes, regions, patterns, levels, or leading diagnoses, whichever organizes the page. Keep it to one page; every worksheet has one, and new worksheets should too.
  - **Part 1, Review:** follows the page's own sections in order, with numbered section headings like the website's, and has no length limit. It stresses how and why, using fill-in-the-blank (`blank`, with `[[answer]]` marks in its `html`, which can hold a table), matching (`match`: `items`, `options`, and the option index for each item), ordering, short answer, multiple choice, and "Select 2".
  - **Part 2, Clinical scenarios:** no standalone questions, because the page's quiz now covers quick multiple-choice practice. Write 2–4 scenarios, by the page's size, until every section of the page is tested. Each is an unfolding case: the presentation, then the workup, a complication, and follow-up, each passage bringing new data. Give results in their real format (biopsy reports, LFT panels, CSF, blood gases, stool tests, imaging reports, trends over days) and ask what they show and what they do not. Prefer free-response decisions with reasons ("next step, and why", "what would change if…", "a colleague suggests X; why not?"), link to other pages where the story leads there, and follow the practice exams' rule against sheer recall. Each scenario has `keyPoints`, a list printed in a box after its answers, and the answers to each scenario start on a new page. Answers and explanations follow, with "Why not the others" for multiple choice. The builder reports the question mix, any justification over its word limit, how often the correct option is longest, and any scenario without key points or leftover `standalone` list. Every GI worksheet has the new Part 2; the neurology worksheets still carry standalone questions in their content files, which the builder no longer prints, and need their scenarios brought up to this standard before they are rebuilt.
- Quiz copies: `node tools/build-quiz-pdf.mjs <gi|neuro|foundations|msk|resp|cardio|endocrine|renal|review> <page id>` writes `<system>/quizzes/<id>.pdf`, an offline copy of the page's quiz: the questions by kind (sets kept together), then every answer with its explanation and notes. An article with `data-quiz-pdf="quizzes/<id>.pdf"` gets an "Offline copy (PDF)" link on its quiz screen. Rebuild it whenever the page's questions change. Every GI condition and special topic page has one, as do the renal pages Metabolic acidosis and Metabolic alkalosis.
- Rewriting a page's quiz: write the page's questions as a block in `tools/quiz-staging/<system>/<id>.js` (the question objects only, as they sit inside the bank's brackets), check it with `node tools/check-quiz-block.mjs <system> <id>` (counts by kind against the target, a note on every wrong option, sets, option balance, American spelling, no letter references), then `node tools/merge-quiz-block.mjs <system> <id>`, which replaces the page's old questions in the bank, keeps the page's place, and adds `data-worksheet` and `data-quiz-pdf` to its article. Delete the staging file once merged; `--merged` checks a page already in the bank. Then build its worksheet and quiz copy, add it to `DONE` in the triage script, and update the landing page's question count.
- Quiz triage: `node tools/triage-quiz.mjs` rewrites `quiz-triage.md`, the working list for the quiz overhaul: each page's questions by kind against its target, how many are still to write, and a verdict on every older question (keep; keep, expand; rewrite; drop). After rewriting a page, add its id to `DONE` in the script and run it again. `--apply` was used once to replace `step` with a proposed kind.
- Page sizes: `node tools/page-sizes.mjs` rewrites `page-sizes.md`, listing every page in sidebar order with its word count (without diagrams, Related line, references, or review article), review article words, diagrams, and questions by kind against the target. Its Change column lists the questions each page should gain or lose under the size rule below. Run it again after adding, merging, splitting, or substantially editing pages.
- Syntax-check a script: `node --check shared/app.js`
- Load a question bank in Node to validate it (it is a plain script, so use `vm`): `vm.runInNewContext(fs.readFileSync("gi/questions.js", "utf8") + ";QUIZ_QUESTIONS")`. Check for unique `id`s, exactly five distinct `options`, `answer` from 0 to 4, a `kind` of memory, reasoning, or analysis, a `condition` matching an article `id`, a note on every wrong option and none on the correct answer, and sets with their passage on the first question only. `tools/check-site.mjs` fails on all of these except the missing notes, which it counts per bank while the older questions are rewritten.
- Browser checks: headless Edge is available at `C:\Program Files (x86)\Microsoft\Edge\Application\msedge.exe`. Headless windows have a 500px minimum width, so test phone layouts with the DevTools protocol's `Emulation.setDeviceMetricsOverride`, and emulate `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` so screenshots are not caught mid-animation.

## Architecture

Each organ system is a folder (`gi/`, `neuro/`, `foundations/`, `msk/`, `resp/`, `cardio/`, `renal/`, `endocrine/`, `review/`) with its own `index.html`, `style.css`, `questions.js` and `map.js`, sharing the engine in `shared/`. Foundations and the review each have one page, with no clinical and physiology split. The root `index.html` is the landing page, styled by `landing.css`; it also redirects old hash links such as `index.html#gerd` to `gi/`. Each system has a panel that uses its own tokens and a large version of its drawing, and a shelf at the bottom holds what comes with it. Each panel shows its topics (condition, special topic, and physiology pages) and questions (both banks), and `tools/check-site.mjs` fails if these, or the "X of Y topics written" line of an unfinished panel (GI, neurology, foundations, musculoskeletal, respiratory, cardiovascular, renal, and endocrine), are out of date; a page counts as written once it loses its last `pending-content` placeholder.

Scripts are classic `defer` scripts sharing one global scope, so load order matters: `shared/app.js`, `questions.js`, `shared/quiz.js`, the organ system's `map.js`, `shared/map.js`, then any organ-specific scripts such as `gi/journey.js` or `neuro/concepts.js`. Top-level `const`s in `app.js` (`conditionButtons`, `contentPanel`, `quizView`, `SITE_NAME` and others) are used directly by later scripts, and redeclaring one is a SyntaxError that stops that script.

- **Physiology pages:** `gi/physiology.html` and `neuro/physiology.html` are second pages of their organ systems, reached by the `.section-switch` in the sidebar. Each uses the same engine and map (GI also the journey), with `data-page="physiology"` on `<body>`. Their quizzes use `physiology-questions.js`, and `data-specialty="gi-physiology"` or `"neuro-physiology"` keeps saved results apart from the clinical quiz. `drawTract` needs every station, so a station with no topic stays in the markup; it keeps its part of the drawing but shows no label or leader line. Links between a system's two pages are plain `<a href="index.html#id">` links, because `data-goto` only reaches the same page. Use `.link-button` in Related lines and `.pair-link` inside a `.pair-list`. `renal/physiology.html` has three groups. The nephron holds Nephron structure (`nephron-structure`, which takes the old `the-nephron` id as an alias), Glomerular filtration, Tubular transport, and Urine concentration and dilution, with clearance formulas; segments keep the numbers 1–8 (glomerulus to collecting duct) on each page, and the Pharmacology group holds placeholders. Homeostasis holds Fluids, then Volume and osmolality regulation (`volume-regulation`, which takes the old `raas-adh-sns` id as an alias and holds the volume-versus-osmolality comparison and water balance moved from Fluids), Renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (`raas`), ADH and thirst (`adh-thirst`), and SNS and natriuretic peptides (`sns-natriuretic`, with prostaglandins), each with receptor tables in the Gastric secretion style, Fluids (compartments and Starling forces), Calcium, phosphate, and magnesium balance (`calcium-balance`: stores, PTH, calcitriol, FGF23, and calcitonin, renal handling with the thick ascending limb, and the CaSR), Potassium balance (where K+ is, the membrane potential, internal balance, and secretion in the collecting duct, holding the compartment composition diagram moved from Fluids), and Acid-base balance (buffers and the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation, the chemoreceptor reflex, renal HCO3− handling and ammoniagenesis, the effects of pH, then a short version of the primary disorders, compensation, and reading a blood gas, ending in When it fails); the renal fluid, electrolyte, and acid-base pages link there instead of repeating that material, and those physiology topics have no "When it fails" section until that clinical group is written.
- **Pages are generated from markup.** `app.js` builds each article's sticky header, with a row of actions beside the title (Review article, Worksheet, then quiz.js's "Quiz me"), jump bar (from `.content-section > h3`) and Previous/Next (from sidebar button order). The header actions are pills of one height, each with an icon (`.header-action-icon`, an inline SVG or the quiz's "?") and a label (`.header-action-label`); phones show the icons alone, keeping the names as accessible labels.
- **Quizzes** are per page. "Quiz me" opens `#quiz/<id>`: a setup screen for that page (which kinds of question, not yet answered or previously missed, and length), then memory questions, reasoning questions, and analysis questions in that order, shuffled within each kind, with a `set` kept together and in order. Feedback shows why the chosen option is wrong, the explanation, then a note on every other option. There is no sidebar quiz button and no quiz across several pages, and the results screen breaks scores down by kind of question.
- **Routing** is by hash, using article `id`s; `#quiz/<id>` opens that page's quiz, and a bare `#quiz` falls back to the first page. A `data-goto` link to a missing article is shown disabled as "not added yet" and activates by itself once that `id` exists. A page that replaced others by merging lists their old ids in `data-aliases` on its article (for example `data-aliases="esophagitis gerd"`), so old links, hashes, and `#quiz/<id>` still land on it; `app.js` resolves them in `resolvePageId`. Saved quiz results keep their question ids, which merges do not change.
- **Sidebar map.** Conditions sit in `.station[data-organ]` groups. `shared/map.js` measures the stations, calls the organ system's `drawTract({ stations, width, add, part, note })`, and draws a dot and leader line for each `[x, y]` point it returns. Every `data-organ` value needs a returned point; the last station sets the drawing height. Redraws follow station size changes, so the drawing must be derived from measured positions, never fixed coordinates.
- **Search** dims non-matches with `.filtered-out` rather than hiding them, so the map keeps its shape (they are hidden only in the phone layout). Keyboard navigation goes through `isListed()`.
- **Phone layout (768px and below):** the map is hidden, `.station` becomes `display: contents`, and conditions form a scrolling pill row.
- **Saved data:** theme in `clinical-theme` (falling back to the older `gi-theme`); quiz results in `<specialty>-quiz-history`, from `<body data-specialty>`. Question `id`s are stored there, so do not rename them.

## Visual style per organ system

Every organ system has its own aesthetic. `shared/style.css` holds structure and the default tokens; an organ system restyles itself in its own `style.css` by overriding tokens and restyling the shared components, keeping the same markup. Token overrides must cover all three selectors the shared file uses: `:root`, `:root:not([data-theme="light"])` inside `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)`, and `:root[data-theme="dark"]`. Anything specific to one organ system stays out of `shared/`.

Neurology's look is "connected": the sidebar is a chain of neurons, each article has one axon running through its section headings, flowchart arrows are axons with boutons, and `neuro/concepts.js` adds a "Shared concepts" panel. Tie conditions together by marking recurring terms with `<span class="concept" data-concept="key">`; the mark is invisible, and a key used across conditions adds the panel to those pages. Lines drawn between mentions, then hover highlighting and click-to-jump between them, were tried and removed.

Review's look is gold with the seven systems' colors braided together (`--sys-` tokens): the sidebar is a braid with a label for each part (`.part-label`, hidden on phones), each article has the cord down its sections, and the landing page mirrors Foundations, with the seven threads weaving into one cord that ends in a book (`braid-` classes in `landing.css`).

Foundations' look is slate with a DNA helix whose rungs carry the seven systems' colors (`--sys-` tokens): the sidebar is the helix, each article has it unwound into a ladder, and the landing page shows a cell whose signal runs from its receptor to the nucleus and out along seven threads to the systems (`cell-` classes in `landing.css`). In every landing animation, a part that resets between loops stays hidden before, during, and after its reset, so the loop never flashes.

Musculoskeletal's look is ivory bone on plum: the sidebar is a spine with a rib at each station, each article has long bones down its sections, jointed at each section number, and the landing page shows an elbow bending and straightening as the biceps and triceps take turns, in flat colors like renal's (`elbow-` classes in `landing.css`; their keyframes are sampled from one bend, so change them together).

Respiratory's look is the bronchial tree, in leaf green: the sidebar is an airway ringed with cartilage that gives off a bronchus and alveoli at each station, each article has the same airway down its sections, ending in an alveolus, and the landing page shows breathing lungs with a flow-volume loop, dark in either theme with air as the light (`lung-` and `loop-` classes in `landing.css`; their keyframes are sampled from one breath, so change them together).

Cardiovascular's look is blood vessels: the sidebar is an artery and a vein joined by a capillary bed at each station, each article has one vessel running from artery (red) through capillary to vein (blue) down its sections, and the landing page shows a beating heart, with its conduction system but no valves or blood flow, beside an ECG, both following one beat on a dark background in either theme, with the signal as the light (`heart-` and `ecg-` classes in `landing.css`; the ECG point's keyframes sample the same curve as the trace, so change them together). On the landing page, Foundations opens and the review closes, each spanning its row; neurology sits half-width on the left before the review, with the space beside it left empty at the site owner's request.

Renal's look is water flowing through a glomerulus and down its tubule: the sidebar is one tubule with a glomerulus at each station, each article has one tubule with water flowing down its sections, and the landing page shows a whole nephron above a graph of the filtrate's osmolality, with one drop on both (`renal-` classes in `landing.css`, sampled from the tubule's path). Endocrine's look is a web of hormones reaching the glands and organs: the sidebar is glands on threads from a central axis, with signals traveling to the open group, each article has hexagonal glands on one thread, and the landing page shows the thyroid feedback loop beside its hormone level settling at a set point, in flat colors (`endo-` classes in `landing.css`, sampled from one curve). Renal articles draw their diagrams (the gut, the blood, and one nephron) with the `rn-` classes in `renal/style.css`.

GI's look is food's journey from the mouth to the rectum. An article with `data-stop` (`esophagus`, `stomach`, `small-intestine`, or `colon`) gets a gut tube down its sections and a bolus that moves down the tube with scrolling, all from `gi/journey.js`. Food does not pass through the accessory organs, so `JOURNEY_BRANCHES` lists `liver`, `biliary`, `pancreas`, and `appendix`, and their tube takes the branch color (`.is-branch`, `--branch-color`). Every GI condition page has this; the special topics pages sit off the tract, so they have no `data-stop`, and the sidebar draws them as a magnifying glass below the rectum.

## Writing content

Neurology's last sidebar group, "Special topics", works bottom-up: Localizing a lesion, Ruling out mimics, and Choosing imaging and EEG (with Localizing weakness and Reading CSF still to write) start from a sign, a mimic, or a test and point to the condition pages. They use their own sections instead of the five condition sections, mostly `If there is | Points to` (or `Consider | Obtain`) tables, pathways, and diagrams. They gather facts already on the condition pages, so keep them in step when those pages change. They have review articles and references like any finished page, and the landing page counts them as topics, and the neuron chain has one neuron per sidebar group.

Renal's sidebar groups are Acute kidney injury (with ATN and AIN), Chronic kidney disease, Glomerular disease, Renal vessels, Cystic and inherited, Urinary tract, Tumors, Water and sodium, Potassium and aldosterone, Calcium, magnesium, phosphate, Acid-base, and Special topics; the notes below describe the written pages, some by their earlier group names. Placeholders still to write: CKD, its complications, dialysis and transplant, `ckd-workup`, PKD, other inherited disease, cystitis (which takes the old `uti` id as an alias), pyelonephritis, stones, urinary tract obstruction (with BPH as a cause only; Postrenal AKI keeps the acute kidney side), tumors, SIADH, diabetes insipidus, aldosterone disorders, phosphate disorders, `respiratory-acid-base`, the five special topics (urinalysis, UECs and urine electrolytes, blood gas, hematuria and proteinuria, and edema, which gather facts from the workup pages), and on the physiology page `transport-defects` (RTA), `kidney-endocrine`, and the pharmacology group (`renal-pharm-*`). One home each: blood gases, RAAS, natriuretic peptides, aldosterone, DKD, and lupus nephritis are renal's; CKD covers hypertensive kidney disease; Foundations has the renal clearance of drugs. Block notes to use when writing: pressure natriuresis and the secondary hypertension workup (block4), Cockcroft-Gault (block1), and the Allen test (block3). The written Fluids and Acid-base balance physiology pages still need their "When it fails" once the electrolyte groups are finished.

Renal's earlier groups are Acute kidney injury (Prerenal, Intrarenal, and Postrenal AKI, written, and `aki-workup`, a comparison and workup page with KDIGO staging, phases, the urine indices, the workup, shared treatment, and the AEIOU dialysis indications, moved there from the volume workup page; intrarenal causes are brief and link to their own pages), Tubulointerstitial injury (ATN and AIN, written, each with its own comparison with the other and no separate workup page; ATN holds rhabdomyolysis and tumor lysis syndrome, and Intrarenal AKI follows their cause lists), then two glomerular groups, all written:

- Nephrotic syndromes: MCD, FSGS, and MN; DKD and amyloid.

**Model condition pages:** Osteoarthritis (`msk/`), Asthma (`resp/`), and Aortic stenosis (`cardio/`) are the reference for every condition page, in content, order, and tone. - **Size:** as long as the condition needs, with no minimum; 2400 words is about the most. Diagrams have no set number: put one wherever it helps most, and none where words or a table do better.
- **Pathophysiology:** one sentence (cause → mechanism → result); a `.pathway`; a subsection with a lettered diagram of the organ or tissue where it helps (`.etiology-map`, letters running on through the section's later diagrams); then mechanisms as diagrams, `.pair-list`s, or tables (a curve or loop where physiology explains it); why each symptom appears; and one note. Comparisons with normal, aging, or endotypes go here.
- **Etiology:** numbered causes, as many as there are, in groups where that helps (primary or risk factors, then secondary or triggers), each a short line of risks or examples, beside a diagram if one helps, and a note. Those numbers are the page's spine.
- **Symptoms and complications:** natural history as a `.pathway` (or the cardinal symptoms as chips), "The pattern" or "Examination" as a `.pair-list`, where it strikes or how severe it is (diagram or table), signs of severity, and "Complications" in a `.complication-grid`.
- **Diagnosis:** one sentence saying what makes the diagnosis; a `.pathway`; the key test explained with its numbers (diagram or severity table); "Other tests" as a `.pair-list`; red flags and "Mimics" (`For | Favored by | Obtain`); then "Find the cause", a two-column `.cause-list` covering every number.
- **Treatment:** one sentence of strategy; a `.pathway` or a table of levels; drugs or procedures with what each does and its place; who to be careful with, as chips; the emergency where there is one; and last "Treat the cause", covering every number. "Guidelines differ:" is the note when major guidelines disagree.
- **Pathways:** 3–7 steps (`.pathway`), each a short noun phrase.
- **Symptom cards:** where a page covers several syndromes or related conditions, `.syndrome-grid` holds one `.syndrome-card` each (a title, a `.node-label`, an optional small `.lesion-map`, and a `.syndrome-facts` list), as on Stroke Syndromes by Location, Red eye, and Nerve palsies; the styles are in `shared/style.css`. Stroke's syndrome diagrams use the slice outlines and lesion shapes from the neighboring `neuro-tracts` project.
- **Form:** every diagram or table is followed by its own short list, entries are phrases of at most about 35 words (pathway details under 14), at most one note per section (`.note`, one sentence, starting `<strong>Note:</strong>` or a bold rule), and no hedging, boxes inside boxes, or repetition between sections. A `.node-label` is a short qualifier of a few words.
- **Numbers and letters:** a cause keeps its number through Etiology, Diagnosis, and Treatment, in order, with every cause covered or marked as covered elsewhere, and is cited by it elsewhere on the page ("(5)"). Causes always take numbers, even when a mnemonic names them (Seizures: "1 Vascular (V)"); letters label effects, mechanism steps, and parts of a diagram. A diagram that does not mark every cause says so in its aria-label.
- **Badges:** `.change.up` and `.change.down` mark Increased and Decreased, `.change.flat` unchanged or normal, and `.change.erratic` variable; renal tables start such cells with a badge (High, Low, Rises, Falls, Normal, Unchanged, Variable).
- **Table headers:** Diagnosis uses `If there is | Obtain` or `For | Obtain`, and mimics `For | Favored by | Obtain`. Treatment uses `Cause | Treatment`, `If there is | Treat with`, `If | Then`, `Goal | Treatment`, or `To | Use`. A comparison table names what it compares.
- **Formulas:** a formula with a fraction is MathML inside `.formula-inline` (`math`, `frac`, `v`, `txt`, `op`, `num`, and `paren` in `tools/page-kit.mjs`), with no library; abbreviations keep their tooltips in their own `<mtext>`, and `.formula-note` defines the symbols. One-line formulas are plain text in the same serif, also in `.formula-inline`.
- **Diagrams:** schematic and static (no hover or click), drawn for the page with an aria-label that says everything in it, in the system's own classes (`ms-`, `rs-`, `cv-`) with tokens for both themes, beside the text they explain. Check them in both themes and for text that overlaps lines.
- **Sources, in order:** the site owner's notes; the guideline they cite, read directly; StatPearls; then PubMed Central reviews, guidelines, and trials. Cite only what was read, and say in the handover which were read only as abstracts.   - "First-line" is always hyphenated, before a noun or after a verb.
  - "Corticosteroids" for the drug class; "glucocorticoids" only in physiology; "most common", not "commonest".
  - "Upper GI bleeding" and "lower GI bleeding", not "upper bleeding" or "upper bleeds".
  - Units are SI: mmHg (no space), mmol/L, µmol/L, g/L; no mg/dL, g/dL, or mEq. Urea, not BUN, and the urea-to-creatinine ratio as UCR (urea in mmol/L ÷ creatinine in mmol/L), high above 100 in prerenal AKI.

### Quizzes

Each page's quiz has two jobs: an Anki-like review of the whole page, short and high in volume, from pathophysiology to management; and teaching, because the explanations are part of the curriculum. The format is described at the top of `gi/questions.js`, and GERD and esophagitis is the model page. The old USMLE-style questions are being replaced page by page from the list in `quiz-triage.md`; every GI condition and special topic page is done, as are the GI physiology pages GI embryology, Gastric secretion, Emetic center, and Liver function; the other GI physiology pages, the GI pharmacology pages, and all neurology pages remain.

- **Counts per page:** quiz length follows page size, so content-heavy pages are not diluted. Every page, of any kind and in every system, has one question per 40 page words (the article's visible text without its diagrams, Related line, references, or review article), rounded to the nearest 5 and kept between 10 and 50, split 2:2:1 into memory, reasoning, and analysis (25 is 10, 10, 5; 50 is 20, 20, 10). Questions are added or removed in groups of 5 (2 memory, 2 reasoning, 1 analysis), and a written quiz changes only when it is 10 or more from its target or outside 10–50, so small edits do not move it. The rule lives in `tools/quiz-target.mjs`, which `tools/check-quiz-block.mjs`, `tools/triage-quiz.mjs`, and `tools/page-sizes.mjs` share; there are no per-page exceptions. Every section of the page should still be tested. Renal and endocrine get their questions as each page is written.
- **Memory** questions are short "what" or "which" recall that becomes reflex: which cancer Barrett esophagus leads to, the most common cause of infectious esophagitis, the finding that favors Crohn disease over ulcerative colitis.
- **Reasoning** questions ask how and why: the mechanism behind a presentation, why a sign appears, how a treatment works, or what to do next (an allergy to one drug in a regimen, a test that comes first).
- **Analysis** questions are a case or data to interpret, relying on inference and familiarity with the page. A case with two or three questions is a `set`, with the `passage` on its first question. Mark invented data as illustrative.
- **Scores and doses** (quizzes, worksheets, practice exams, and exam forms alike):
  - Never ask for a score to be calculated or its criteria and points to be recalled (CHA₂DS₂-VA, MELD, Child-Pugh, Leipzig, and others), such as "given these findings, what is the Child-Pugh score?" Asking what a score is for is fine ("which score ranks patients for liver transplant?"), as are its concept and limits, and what a given score means for the next step. A scenario can give the score, and a worksheet or exam can supply its criteria as supplementary material.
  - Do not test a drug's dosage or time course. Route is fine to test (PO or IV vancomycin). When a multiple-choice option mentions a dose or duration, it is always the correct one for that drug, in every option, wrong ones included, so no option is wrong only because of its dose or duration.
  - Decision limits are not doses and can be tested: treatment windows (thrombolysis within 4.5 hours, NAC within 8, thrombectomy to 24) and, sparingly, monitoring targets (INR 2–3, ferritin under 50 µg/L).
  - Criteria that are not scores (Rome V, Duke, Ottawa SAH, Light, NIAAA) are not tested by recall or by counting items; apply them to a case when they are supplied, or ask conceptually what makes the diagnosis.
  - Formulas (anion gap, FENa, Winter, clearance) can be calculated when the formula is supplied; Cockcroft-Gault is asked only conceptually.
  - Give the normal range with every lab value that has one (pH, Na+, PaCO2, HCO3−, HbA1c, and others). A value with no established range can be marked "high" or "low" instead.
  - These rules apply to new work only; older questions are left as they are until a page is rewritten.
- **No traps.** No deliberately vague wording, wordplay, or long options with one small mistake; distractors and red herrings are fine. Avoid "EXCEPT" and "NOT" lead-ins.
- **Explanations** teach the why and how: why the answer is right, why it is the guideline, and how the mechanism leads there, with short HTML lists where they help. `notes` covers every wrong option, saying why it is wrong or when it would be right. Guideline differences go in `info`.
- **The sample bank** in the neighboring `review` folder (`../review/questions.js`, 360 questions) sets the tone. A relevant question can be borrowed for its page, converted to American spelling and the site's terms, with its KAT and Paper references removed, notes added for every wrong option, and its facts checked against the page.
- **Options** are shuffled when shown, so never refer to an option by its letter or position. Keep them similar in length and detail, so the correct one is the longest in about one question in five; `tools/check-site.mjs` fails above 30%. Write options in plain text; stems, passages, explanations, notes, and `info` may hold HTML.
