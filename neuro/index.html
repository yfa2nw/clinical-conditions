Cerebral ischemic syndromes

ACA syndrome

Artery
Anterior cerebral artery
Motor
Contralateral leg weakness, greater than arm
Sensory
Contralateral leg loss
Other
Urinary incontinence, abulia, grasp reflex

MCA syndrome

dominant hemisphere
Artery
Middle cerebral artery
Motor
Contralateral face and arm weakness, greater than leg; gaze toward the lesion
Sensory
Contralateral face and arm loss
Other
Aphasia, contralateral homonymous hemianopia, Gerstmann syndrome

MCA syndrome

non-dominant hemisphere
Artery
Middle cerebral artery
Motor
Contralateral face and arm weakness, greater than leg; gaze toward the lesion
Sensory
Contralateral face and arm loss; extinction
Other
Hemispatial neglect, anosognosia, contralateral homonymous hemianopia

PCA syndrome

Artery
Posterior cerebral artery
Motor
Usually none
Sensory
Contralateral loss if the thalamus is involved
Other
Contralateral homonymous hemianopia with macular sparing; alexia without agraphia (dominant); prosopagnosia (non-dominant)

MCA/ACA watershed syndrome

"man in a barrel" if bilateral
Cause
Watershed infarct from low perfusion or ICA stenosis
Motor
Proximal arm and shoulder weakness; hand and face spared
Sensory
Mild proximal arm loss
Other
Transcortical motor aphasia (dominant)

MCA/PCA watershed syndrome

Cause
Watershed infarct from low perfusion or ICA stenosis
Motor
Usually none
Sensory
Usually mild
Other
Visual field loss; transcortical sensory aphasia (dominant); neglect (non-dominant); Balint syndrome if bilateral

Intracerebral hemorrhage

Features

  • Headache
  • Cortical focal neurologic deficits

Raised ICP

  • Nausea and vomiting
  • Loss of consciousness
  • Posturing
  • Cushing triad

Complications

  • Cerebral edema malignant MCA infarct, herniation within 2–5 days
  • Hemorrhagic transformation
  • Aspiration pneumonia dysphagia
  • Seizures
  • DVT and PE immobility
  • Spasticity and contractures
  • Post-stroke depression
  • Central post-stroke pain thalamic infarct

Treatment

Acute treatment

GoalTreatment
Reperfusion
  • IV alteplase or tenecteplase within 4.5 hours of onset
  • Mechanical thrombectomy for large vessel occlusion up to 24 hours with penumbra
AntiplateletAspirin within 24–48 hours wait 24 hours after thrombolysis
Supportive care
  • Swallow screen before oral intake
  • Treat fever and glucose
  • DVT prophylaxis

Blood pressure: below 185/110 for thrombolysis; otherwise allow up to 220/120.

Thrombolysis contraindications

  • Hemorrhage on CT
  • Stroke or head trauma within 3 months
  • Intracranial or spinal surgery within 3 months
  • Active bleeding
  • Platelets under 100000/µL
  • INR over 1.7
  • Infective endocarditis

CauseTreatment
Cardiac embolusAnticoagulation DOAC; warfarin for mechanical valve
Infective endocarditisABX

Small vessel thrombusAntiplatelet, statin, blood pressure control

VasculitisImmunosuppression; treat any infection
DissectionAntiplatelet or anticoagulation

Intracerebral Hemorrhage

Pathophysiology

Vessel rupture bleeds into the brain parenchyma, forming a hematoma that compresses tissue and raises ICP.

Etiology

Hypertension Most common under 60

  • Basal ganglia Important
  • Pons
  • Cerebellum
  • Cortex less common

Cerebral amyloid angiopathy Most common over 60

  • Cortical structures may present with dementia

Coagulopathy

  • Warfarin
  • Heparin
  • DOACs
  • tPA
  • Aspirin
  • Clopidogrel
  • Liver disease

Hemorrhagic transformation

  • Ischemic stroke
  • Conversion from tPA
  • Reperfusion injury

Arteriovenous malformation

  • Vascular abnormality young patients

Mycotic aneurysm

  • Infective endocarditis
  • Vegetation deposits

Cerebral venous sinus thrombosis

Factor V Leiden
Decreased AT-III
Decreased protein C or S
APS
OCP
Pregnancy
Venous sinus thrombus
Sinus swelling
Hemorrhage near the cortex

Malignancy

Papillary thyroid cancer
Small cell lung carcinoma
RCC
Melanoma
Choriocarcinoma
Disrupted BBB
Blood around the tumor

Diagnosis

First Non-contrast CT head hyperdense blood in the parenchyma
If young, lobar, or no hypertension CT angiogram look for a vascular cause

Find the cause

ForObtain
HypertensionNon-contrast CT head deep location
Cerebral amyloid angiopathyMRI with SWI lobar microbleeds
CoagulopathyFBC, PT/INR, aPTT, LFTs
Hemorrhagic transformationCT head after stroke or thrombolysis blood within the infarct
Cerebral venous sinus thrombosisCT or MR venography empty delta sign
MalignancyMRI with contrast enhancing mass
Arteriovenous malformationCT angiogram catheter angiography to confirm
Mycotic aneurysmBlood cultures, echocardiogram, CT angiogram looking for infective endocarditis

Note: The ICH score estimates 30-day mortality.

Treatment

Acute treatment

GoalTreatment
Limit hematoma growth
  • IV nicardipine, clevidipine, or labetalol
  • Reverse any coagulopathy
Lower ICP
  • Head of bed at 30°
  • Hypertonic saline or mannitol
  • EVD hydrocephalus
DecompressionSurgical evacuation cerebellar bleed over 3 cm
Supportive care
  • ICU admission
  • Treat fever and hyperglycemia
  • Antiseizure medication only for seizures
  • Intermittent pneumatic compression

Blood pressure: if systolic is 150–220, lower it to a target of 140.

Reverse coagulopathy

If there isTreat with
Warfarin4-factor PCC and IV vitamin K
HeparinProtamine
DabigatranIdarucizumab
Apixaban or rivaroxabanAndexanet alfa or 4-factor PCC
tPACryoprecipitate
Aspirin or clopidogrelStop the offending drug platelets only before neurosurgery
Liver diseaseVitamin K and FFP

CauseTreatment
HypertensionLong-term control below 130/80
Cerebral amyloid angiopathyAvoid antithrombotics where possible
Hemorrhagic transformationStop antithrombotics
