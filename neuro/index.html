Intracerebral hemorrhage

Features

  • Headache
  • Cortical focal neurologic deficits

Raised ICP

  • Nausea and vomiting
  • Loss of consciousness
  • Posturing
  • Cushing triad

Complications

  • Cerebral edema malignant MCA infarct, herniation within 2–5 days
  • Hemorrhagic transformation
  • Aspiration pneumonia dysphagia
  • Seizures
  • DVT and PE immobility
  • Spasticity and contractures
  • Post-stroke depression
  • Central post-stroke pain thalamic infarct

Acute Ischemic Stroke

Pathophysiology

Blocked or inadequate arterial blood flow leads to cerebral ischemia, causing an infarct core surrounded by a salvageable penumbra.

Etiology

  • Embolus Most common
  • 1Cardiac embolus ImportantLeft atrial thrombus: A-fib or flutter, mechanical valve, or RHD
    Left ventricular thrombus: LV aneurysm from MI, anterior or lateral MI, or HFrEF
  • 2Infective endocarditisCardiac embolus; a contraindication to thrombolysis
  • 3Paradoxical embolusASD, patent foramen ovale, or atrial septal aneurysm; a right-to-left shunt lets a venous thrombus reach arterial circulation
  • Thrombus
  • 4Large vessel thrombusAtherosclerotic thrombus in the ICA, MCA, or vertebrobasilar arteries
  • 5Small vessel thrombusLacunar infarct from lenticulostriate arteries or small pontine branches
  • Low flow
  • 6Global hypoperfusionCardiac arrest, acute respiratory failure, or ICA stenosis with low BP; decreased cerebral oxygen delivery causes watershed infarcts
  • Vessel wall
  • 7VasculitisAutoimmune, infections, or primary CNS vasculitis; arterial wall inflammation
  • 8DissectionTrauma, iatrogenic, or connective tissue diseases; intimal tear with thrombus and embolus

Diagnosis

  1. Glucose and non-contrast CT headRule out hypoglycemia and hemorrhage
  2. CT angiogram of the head and neckLook for large vessel occlusion
  3. CT perfusion or MRIIf over 6 hours or onset unknown; look for penumbra

Find the cause

  • 1Cardiac embolusECG, telemetry, and echocardiogram: A-fib, thrombus
  • 2Infective endocarditisBlood cultures and echocardiogram
  • 3Paradoxical embolusBubble echocardiogram: PFO
  • 4Large vessel thrombusCT angiogram or carotid duplex: ICA stenosis
  • 5Small vessel thrombusMRI: lacunar infarct
  • 7VasculitisESR, CRP, autoimmune serology, and CSF
  • 8DissectionCT or MR angiogram of the neck
  • +Vascular risk factorsLipids and HbA1c

Note: NIHSS grades stroke severity.

Treatment

Acute treatment

  • IV alteplase or tenecteplase within 4.5 hours of onset
  • Mechanical thrombectomy for large vessel occlusion, up to 24 hours with penumbra
  • Aspirin within 24–48 hours; wait 24 hours after thrombolysis
Onset4.5 hours24 hours48 hours
Supportive care
  • Swallow screen before oral intake
  • Treat fever and glucose
  • DVT prophylaxis

Blood pressure: below 185/110 for thrombolysis; otherwise allow up to 220/120.

Thrombolysis contraindications

  • Hemorrhage on CT
  • Stroke or head trauma within 3 months
  • Intracranial or spinal surgery within 3 months
  • Active bleeding
  • Platelets under 100000/µL
  • INR over 1.7
  • Infective endocarditis

Prevent recurrence

  • 1Cardiac embolusAnticoagulation: DOAC; warfarin for mechanical valve
  • 2Infective endocarditisABX
  • 3Paradoxical embolusAntiplatelet; PFO closure if under 60 with no other cause
  • 4Large vessel thrombusAntiplatelet and high-intensity statin; carotid endarterectomy for symptomatic ICA stenosis of 70–99%
  • 5Small vessel thrombusAntiplatelet, statin, and BP control
  • 6Global hypoperfusionTreat the cause of low perfusion
  • 7VasculitisImmunosuppression; treat any infection
  • 8DissectionAntiplatelet or anticoagulation

Intracerebral Hemorrhage

Pathophysiology

Vessel rupture bleeds into the brain parenchyma, forming a hematoma that compresses tissue and raises ICP.

Etiology

  • Deep
  • 1Hypertension Most common under 60Basal ganglia Important, pons, and cerebellum; cortex is less common
  • Cortex
  • 2Cerebral amyloid angiopathy Most common over 60Cortical bleeds; may present with dementia
  • 3Cerebral venous sinus thrombosisFactor V Leiden, decreased AT-III, decreased protein C or S, APS, OCP, and pregnancy; sinus swelling near the cortex
  • Any site
  • 4CoagulopathyWarfarin, heparin, DOACs, tPA, aspirin, clopidogrel, and liver disease
  • 5Hemorrhagic transformationIschemic stroke conversion from tPA or reperfusion injury
  • 6MalignancyPapillary thyroid cancer, small cell lung carcinoma, RCC, melanoma, and choriocarcinoma disrupt the BBB
  • 7Arteriovenous malformationVascular abnormality in young patients
  • 8Mycotic aneurysmInfective endocarditis vegetation deposits

Diagnosis

  1. Non-contrast CT headHyperdense blood in the parenchyma
  2. BloodsFBC, PT/INR, aPTT, and LFTs
  3. CT angiogramIf young, lobar, or no hypertension

Find the cause

  • 1HypertensionNon-contrast CT head: deep location
  • 2Cerebral amyloid angiopathyMRI with SWI: lobar microbleeds
  • 3Cerebral venous sinus thrombosisCT or MR venography: empty delta sign
  • 4CoagulopathyFBC, PT/INR, aPTT, and LFTs
  • 5Hemorrhagic transformationCT head after stroke or thrombolysis: blood within the infarct
  • 6MalignancyMRI with contrast: enhancing mass
  • 7Arteriovenous malformationCT angiogram; catheter angiography to confirm
  • 8Mycotic aneurysmBlood cultures, echocardiogram, and CT angiogram

Note: The ICH score estimates 30-day mortality.

Treatment

Acute treatment

Limit hematoma growth
  • IV nicardipine, clevidipine, or labetalol
  • Reverse any coagulopathy
Lower ICP
  • Head of bed at 30°
  • Hypertonic saline or mannitol
  • EVD for hydrocephalus
Decompression
  • Surgical evacuation for a cerebellar bleed over 3 cm
Supportive care
  • ICU admission
  • Treat fever and hyperglycemia
  • Antiseizure medication only for seizures
  • Intermittent pneumatic compression

Blood pressure: if systolic is 150–220, lower it to a target of 140.

Reverse coagulopathy

  • Warfarin4-factor PCC and IV vitamin K
  • HeparinProtamine
  • DabigatranIdarucizumab
  • Apixaban or rivaroxabanAndexanet alfa or 4-factor PCC
  • tPACryoprecipitate
  • Aspirin or clopidogrelStop the offending drug; platelets only before neurosurgery
  • Liver diseaseVitamin K and FFP

Treat the cause

  • 1HypertensionLong-term control below 130/80
  • 2Cerebral amyloid angiopathyAvoid antithrombotics where possible
  • 3Cerebral venous sinus thrombosisAnticoagulation, even with hemorrhage
  • 4CoagulopathyReverse as above
  • 5Hemorrhagic transformationStop antithrombotics
  • 6MalignancySurgery, radiation, or systemic therapy
  • 7Arteriovenous malformationSurgical resection, embolization, or radiosurgery
  • 8Mycotic aneurysmIV ABX for infective endocarditis; endovascular or surgical repair if ruptured
